## The big picture

Think of an assistant that answers a question such as "which facilities had the highest
readmission rate last quarter?" and then offers to open a follow-up task.

```text
 person ──asks──▶ Foundry agent (reasoning, orchestration)
                       │
                       │ calls tools through MCP (a standard plug)
                       ▼
                 Fabric (governed context: tables, semantic model, data agent)
                       │
                       ▼
        answer + evidence (traces, evaluations, audit)
                       │
 person ◀──proposal── agent: "open a follow-up task?"
                       │ human approves (Entra identity + policy)
                       ▼
                 business system (records the task)
```

## Five ideas to remember

1. **Fabric = context.** Fabric holds the data and its meaning (a semantic model says what
   "readmission rate" is) and enforces who can see which rows.
2. **Foundry = reasoning and action.** A Foundry agent decides which tool to call, combines the
   results, explains them and proposes next steps.
3. **MCP = the plug, not the key.** The Model Context Protocol is a standard way for an AI
   client to find and call tools. Having a plug does not give you the right to use it.
4. **Identity = the key.** Microsoft Entra ID identifies who is asking. Fabric roles, policy and
   human approval decide what that identity may do.
5. **The business system = the record.** Analytics never becomes the system of record. Writes go
   to the system that owns the transaction, and only after approval.

## Two more that keep it honest

- **Evidence.** Traces, evaluations and audit records show what actually happened. "The tool
  exists" or "the item exists" is not evidence that something ran.
- **Offline resilience.** This accelerator runs the whole story on a laptop with synthetic data.
  Every result carries a label such as `LOCAL` or `SIMULATED`, so nobody confuses a rehearsal
  with a real cloud operation.

## Glossary

- **Governed context:** data plus definitions plus permissions.
- **Orchestration:** deciding which steps and tools to use, in which order.
- **Authority:** the right to perform an action, granted by identity and policy.
- **Execution label:** the tag on every result that says where it really ran.
