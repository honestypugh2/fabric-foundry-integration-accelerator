## What it is

A table represents facts at a particular **grain**. One row might be an order line, not an order.
A key identifies a record; a relationship describes how records connect. A measure gives an
agreed calculation and its context.

## Why this leads to a lakehouse

Bronze preserves source values. Silver applies types, keys and quality rules. Gold presents
usable business facts. A semantic model adds reusable meaning. More layers do not automatically
make data correct: each transition needs an explicit contract and checks.

## Worked example

Two order lines share one order key. Their revenue can be added; counting both rows as two
orders is wrong. Joining a line to two matching dimension rows can double its revenue again.
Ask Copilot to explain the grain before asking it to generate a query.
